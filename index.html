Teaching notes
  • Why numerals. A numeral names a chord by its office in the key, not its letter. I–V–vi–IV is one shape in every key — so a song in G and a song in D become the same lesson.
  • Reading the plate. Capitals are major (I, IV, V), lowercase minor (ii, iii, vi), lowercase with ° diminished (vii°). Stroke weight follows: heavy major, fine minor, broken diminished. Height rises with the degree.
  • Start here. Hide the names, play I, IV and V only, and let them hear home / away / tension before the minors are introduced.
  • The transfer test. Once C is secure, change the key to G and repeat. If the numerals still hold, the idea has landed — that is the whole point of Week 4.
  • On a phone. Add it to the homescreen and it opens like an app and works with no signal. On iPhone the first tap unlocks audio, and the silent switch must be off.
